# RVA Builds — Tone & Voice

> How RVA Builds sounds in writing: headlines, social captions, emails, press materials, and signage.

## Voice attributes

| Trait | What it means | Sounds like | Not this |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hopeful** | Lead with what's possible, not what's broken. | "A choice-filled life is the standard." | "Too many kids fall through the cracks." (fear-first) |
| **Trustworthy** | Back claims with real numbers and named partners. | "500 students, 17 partners, by 2028–29." | Vague superlatives ("the best program ever") |
| **Civic** | This is Richmond's initiative, for Richmond's kids — write like a neighbor, not a brand. | "Right here in Richmond, Virginia." | Generic, placeless corporate copy |
| **Warm** | People first. A student, a mentor, a mayor — never an abstraction. | "A student earning a paycheck and a future." | "Human capital," "stakeholders," "the pipeline" (as the subject) |
| **Data-informed** | Specific numbers, cited plainly, never inflated. | "$23/hour minimum total compensation after graduation." | Rounding up, or stats without a source |
| **Editorial** | Reads like a well-edited feature story, not an ad. | Full sentences, active voice, real quotes. | Jargon strings, buzzword stacking, exclamation points |

## Writing rules

- **Sentence case for headlines.** Civic and warm, not shouty. Never full caps except in eyebrow labels/kickers.
- **Second person when talking to candidates or employers directly** — "you," not "students" or "employers" in direct address.
- **Active voice, short sentences.** If a sentence needs a semicolon, it's two sentences.
- **Numbers as proof, not decoration.** Every stat should be sourced and specific (see `boilerplate.md`). Don't say "hundreds" if the real number is 500.
- **Name people.** Quote real leaders with real titles. Avoid "officials say."
- **Plain language over jargon.** Say "paid, on-the-job training" before "work-based learning" on first reference; define acronyms once.
- **Two audiences, one program.** Keep Candidate copy (purple) and Employer copy (blue) equally weighted — never make one path feel like an afterthought.
- **Don't oversell.** No "revolutionary," "game-changing," or "world-class." Let the outcomes (wages, hours, partners) do the persuading.

## Audience-specific voice

**Talking to candidates (students / families)** — encouraging, concrete, low-jargon:
> "Get paid to learn a trade — before you even graduate. Electrical, HVAC, welding, construction: real hours, real wages, real employers in Richmond."

**Talking to employers / partners** — direct, ROI-minded, still warm:
> "Train your next hire while they're still in school. RVA Builds connects you with RPS students ready for paid, on-the-job experience — and a clear path to a Registered Apprenticeship."

**Talking to press / civic partners** — editorial, sourced, quote-forward:
> "Backed by Bloomberg Philanthropies, RVA Builds is Richmond Ed Fund's citywide push to connect Richmond Public Schools students with paid apprenticeship pathways in the skilled trades."

## Sample messaging

- **Tagline direction:** *Building Richmond's next workforce.*
- **One-line pitch:** *RVA Builds pairs RPS students with paid apprenticeship pathways into Richmond's skilled trades.*
- **Mission framing:** *A choice-filled life isn't a slogan — it's what RVA Builds is built to deliver.*

## Do / Don't quick reference

**Do**
- Write like you're explaining the program to a neighbor over coffee.
- Lead with a real student, employer, or leader — not a statistic.
- Use "apprenticeship," "Registered Apprenticeship," and "paid work-based learning" precisely and correctly.

**Don't**
- Don't use deficit language about Richmond, RPS, or students ("struggling," "at-risk," "failing schools").
- Don't imply a guarantee of a job or wage the program itself doesn't make.
- Don't mix the two grotesk typefaces' roles — see `typography.md` for which face carries which voice (Space Grotesk = confident/declarative, Schibsted Grotesk = conversational/informative).
